# Lore Prose

Lore is the narrative register: the history of a species or a tree, told the way a roleplaying book
tells it. Write it through the `aubaine-lore` skill: `.claude/skills/aubaine-lore/references/lore.md`
holds the craft and its examples; read it before drafting or revising a lore file.
`docs/runbooks/add-a-species.md` owns the file format.

- Start from the owner's text when there is one. Move and retitle it, and change its words only where
  the owner's words name the passage, or for slips, typography and canon that has moved. An existing
  page is revised only when the owner's words ask for it, and then only as far as those words reach.
- Owner text is what the owner wrote for the page
  (`.claude/skills/aubaine-lore/references/lore.md` § Start from the owner's text), never what is
  committed. No sentence goes on a page of the owner's that the owner's words do not ask for.
- Read every lore page that shares a people, a place or an event before writing, and follow the
  newest canon and `.claude/skills/aubaine-lore/references/rulings.md`.
- Invent no fact about the world. In `write` with no owner text, list every narrative inference in
  the report for the owner to veto; anywhere else an inference is a proposal, off the page.
- A fact has one home inside « Histoire », and one inside the opening and « En jeu »; another part on
  the same side names it and adds only a new consequence. Across the two sides a fact may be restated
  lightly, and a section is never retold. A tree page has no play part, so it is one side
  (`.claude/skills/aubaine-lore/references/architecture.md` § One home per fact).
- A species page is about its own people. Another people enters only through this people's view of
  it, a bond with it, or one clause of contrast that ends on this people, never in a paragraph about
  itself.
- Borrow no name, joke, label or mechanic from another game. Never name one, and never frame a page
  against one.
- A belief keeps its holder, a dispute its accounts and a mystery its silence. Never fill an unknown.
- Dialogue carries its emotion in the lines, never in stage directions.
- The English edition is written for an English reader, with the same claims and the same certainty
  as the French.
