# Hextech, League of Legends

Hextech, in this file, is the interface language Riot Games built for the League of Legends client
update: gold metal linework, blue-black grounds under painted art, and blue Hextech magic kept for the
game's own actions, its timers and its progress. On 2026-10-29 the owner named it among Aubaine's three
sources: "Imagine a mix between Deadlock, The Persona Series (more preciesly Persona 5) and the Hextech
from League of Legends."

It gives Aubaine the rules for its gold and its light. It does not give a device: reading the client as
one led to the CADRAN screen, which stays with the Atlas boot ([What Aubaine leaves](#what-aubaine-leaves)).
The owner's rulings and their dates are in `rulings.md`, and `DESIGN.md` documents the look; this file
holds the Hextech evidence behind them.

Other studios' screens are linked here and never copied into the repository; screenshots of Aubaine's
accepted pieces are in `../assets/approved/`. Every link below answered on 2026-10-30 and again on
2026-10-31, and every screen this file calls viewed was looked at on 2026-10-30.

## Scope and dates

- **The client.** The League Wiki's [Client](https://wiki.leagueoflegends.com/en-us/Client) page: "It
  released alongside Season 2017, replacing the old Adobe AIR application which used the PVP.net
  networking interface." Riot's
  [2017 Season Update](https://na.leagueoflegends.com/en/featured/preseason-2017/client): "Hundreds of
  thousands of alpha testers helped us rebuild the League client from the ground up", and "We also took
  the opportunity to rework the client's visuals with a cohesive (and kinda sexy) hextech design."
- **The name.** Riot's foundations team, then defining Piltover, had concept art of a technology it
  called Hextech; the client's language grew from it ([the primary source](#the-primary-source)).
- **Not studied here.** Arcane's look, Hextech-themed skins, the chests and keys of Hextech Crafting (a
  loot system launched in March 2016, before the client update shipped), the in-match interface, and
  League's marketing sites: the owner wants "the feel of those Video game", not "the feel of a Video Game
  Webiste" (2026-10-28).
- **It has moved.** Patch 12.1
  ([Riot, 4 January 2022](https://www.leagueoflegends.com/en-us/news/game-updates/patch-12-1-notes/)):
  "Ranked Armor has transformed into Ranked Crests!", "Ranked Crests also have ~*ambient magic*~!",
  "Several parts of the game lobby UI have been rearranged for clarity." On 25 November 2024 Riot
  announced a new season model with a Battle Pass
  ([wiki](https://wiki.leagueoflegends.com/en-us/Battle_Pass)). The Client page, last edited on
  12 June 2026: "Client was heavily redesigned around the 2025 Annual Cycle (info hub, new home layout,
  Sanctum)." GameDaily reported on 1 January 2026 that Riot is "working on a new around game client
  that's fully integrated with League of Legends' in-game experience"
  ([via Yahoo](https://tech.yahoo.com/gaming/articles/league-legends-client-coming-2026-021219350.html)),
  and the Client page says: "In 2027, the LCU is set to be superseded by a complete client refactoring
  that hosts all aspects of League of Legends in one application, called League Next." Every screen
  below carries its date for that reason.

## The primary source

Riot Games, "The Visual Language of Hextech", on Riot's Nexus site, December 2016. Its address,
`nexus.leagueoflegends.com/en-us/2016/12/the-visual-language-of-hextech`, now redirects to the League home
page. Two copies were read:

- Surrender at 20's
  [Red Post Collection of 2 January 2017](https://www.surrenderat20.net/2017/01/red-post-collection-2016-playlist.html),
  with the text and eleven figures, the three shapes among them. All eleven figures were viewed.
- [An unattributed repost](https://loltopchamp.wordpress.com/2017/09/26/the-visual-language-of-hextech/)
  of 26 September 2017, with the three shapes as text.

What it sets down, in its own words:

- **Why.** The team wanted "a visual style that felt like a fantasy game, but avoided the normal fantasy
  game UI cliches." "Parchment and other distressed elements that littered the client in the past look
  dated relatively quickly", and the language had to "scale over multiple features and mediums" without
  "a dedicated visual artist (or more than one)" on every team.
- **A question, answered with a language.** Thematically, Hextech "served as a way of democratizing
  magic to those who didn’t have those abilities naturally." The team asked: "What is the client itself
  was like a Hextech tool that the players could use to interact with the magical world of the game?"
  The question became "the stake in the ground that we built the user interface around", and what came
  out of it was a set of shapes and colours for every screen, not a device drawn on one. Riot's Ask Riot
  of October 2016 (mirrored by Surrender at 20 on
  [29 October 2016](https://www.surrenderat20.net/2016/10/red-post-collection-ask-riot-on-lcu.html))
  gives the reason: "there's no visual consistency between pages. With the update, we’ve redesigned it
  with Hextech magic for a cohesive visual experience that fits the look and feel of League of Legends."
- **Three core shapes**, each with a job:
  - the square, STRUCTURE: "Used to establish stability to elements and reinforce our grid";
  - the diamond, PROGRESSIVE: "An accent piece used to help guide the eye towards key information";
  - the circle, FOCUS: "Built to draw focus towards primary items that require a reaction".

  Together they "bring an ornate decorative framing to key areas of attention" and "draw the eye towards
  a focal point".
- **Magic** shows up "in many different ways, such as timers, activity indicators, and progress bars",
  under three tenets:
  - VISCOUS: "Hextech magic tends to stick around and has some weight to it, falling somewhere in between
    water and smoke."
  - DIRECTIONAL: "Always moving towards a particular direction, the intelligent aspect of the magic is
    geared towards guiding people."
  - VALUE: "The highest values should be used to attract the player’s attention, saving brighter
    executions for pivotal moments in the experience."
- **Two layers.** The interface layer: "the use of metal linework lives on the top layer of the client,
  and acts as a framing element to support and draw focus towards key pieces of information." The
  interactive layer has three areas, "Gameplay, Secondary & Utility":
  - blue Hextech magic buttons "appear sparingly throughout the client" and are kept for actions
    "relating to the game itself";
  - "Gold buttons that appear throughout the client work to provide interactions in and out of secondary
    information. Navigating through a carousel, closing out of a modal window, and purchasing items all
    have a similar look and style to them."
  - "Sorting, filtering, and tooltips all fall into this layer of interface interaction."
- **Beyond the client.** The article ends on Hextech "seeding their way into the world through a number of
  different mediums"; its last figure shows the language on a web page, a phone app, a book and a
  collector's box.

## How Riot built it

Three articles by Riot engineers say how the look was made.

- **A web app.** Andrew McVeigh
  ([16 May 2016](https://www.riotgames.com/en/news/architecture-league-client-update)): the old client was
  built in 2008 on Adobe AIR, and "we chose to start again and build around Chromium Embedded Framework
  (CEF) as our front-end." Jules Glegg
  ([14 February 2017](https://www.riotgames.com/en/news/under-hood-league-client%E2%80%99s-hextech-ui)):
  front-end plugins "are written as Javascript client applications and run inside Chromium Embedded
  Framework"; past their bootstrap, "it’s really all Just Web"; the shared buttons, dropdown menus,
  tooltips and dialogs are "straight-up native Custom Elements with heavy usage of Shadow DOM". Richard Ye
  ([27 February 2018](https://www.riotgames.com/en/news/animation-league-legends-client)) adds that the
  client is not a traditional web app: at its core it is "a game UI with high-quality visuals".
- **One set of timings and easings.** Glegg: "For consistency, we distribute a shared Stylus mixin with
  preset timings and easing functions to give animations that Hextech feel, client-wide. Providing
  different easing functions allows animations to convey priority." In the article's example, pieces
  "land softly in position", or "snap into place to grab your attention and communicate urgency". Simpler
  animations are CSS animations and transitions.
- **Magic baked into small videos, played as states.** Glegg: Riot built "a state machine library based on
  Web Components that can be used to create simple groups of video with state-to-state transitions for
  hover, click, and other events"; "Video is great for ethereal, magical effects or highly-detailed
  mechanical animations". The article's example is the ready check's countdown ring: an intro clip when
  the player accepts, then an idle loop that "plays continuously while we wait for other players to
  accept the matchup". Ye: the PLAY text "is rendered by the browser in the user’s chosen language, while
  the rest of the button consists of multiple videos for the various states"; such videos are "often 100
  kilobytes or less"; "our Hextech UI includes a lot of animated smoke and “magic” effects which are
  difficult to render efficiently in real-time with high quality", and "As of now, WebGL is just not a
  good fit for the past and present needs of the League Client UI."
- **Cheap to draw.** Ye, on Chromium: "we should try to only animate the opacity and transform
  properties." The client's "low-spec mode replaces most large videos with static images". The ten-ban
  animation of champion select moved from hand-timed CSS to GSAP timelines.

## What Aubaine takes

Each move says where it already lives in the approved pieces (the character sheet, approved on 2026-10-27,
and the select screen of Arbres and Espèces, approved on 2026-10-28, both at commit `a9b3954`; the Codex's
plate menu and the Livres contents, approved on 2026-10-31; `approved-pieces.md` holds all four), or that it is a rule for new
pieces. Primitives are under `src/components/primitives/`.

1. **Ornament only on the focal or celebratory piece.** In the 2025 Collection the one ornate frame (cut
   corners, large gold numerals) holds the mastery tally, only the pointed card gets a pale gold hairline,
   and the tabs and filters stay plain. Aubaine's approved pieces make the focal card with its size, its
   painting, its slab and the stage's light, not with a heavier frame: every card, pointed or not, wears
   the same two flat 1 px rules in its key colour, at 60 % and 28 % (`FramedCard.astro`), which read gold
   only when the key falls back to house gold (`KEY_FALLBACK` in `src/lib/key-colour.ts`). For new
   pieces: one celebratory piece at most, and never a tally the list below already gives. The owner,
   2026-10-27: "what's the point of having the number of each if it is shown afterwards with the number
   of results ?"
2. **A value ladder of one gold.** Riot: "The highest values should be used to attract the player’s
   attention". An unattributed community palette
   ([Colors, League of Legends](https://vitorsammarco.github.io/league-of-legends-css-styles/demo/colors.html),
   not confirmed by Riot) names the steps: structural borders in a dark gold (Gold 5, `#785A28`), text and
   icons in buttons in Gold 2, hover states and headers in Gold 1, disabled elements in Grey 2. Aubaine
   climbs the same ladder with one vibrant gold: the step, help and pause squares and the filter fields
   rest under `--line2` (house gold `#efbe04` at 26 %, `src/styles/tokens.css`) and their border turns
   full `--gold` when pointed or focused; a pointed roster card turns to -0.6 times its resting tilt,
   lifts 6 px and swings its slab out (`CoverCard.astro`, `FramedCard.astro`); the focal card takes the
   stage's light. The faint end is thinness and transparency, never a browner gold: the owner asked for a
   more vibrant gold when a softened one read as dark and dusty
   (`docs/adr/0059-a-sub-species-may-fly-its-heraldry-as-cloth-and-a-painting-hangs-under-glass.md`,
   Context). The dark gold `#7a5d10` is used only in print (`CharacterHero.astro`, `SelectScreen.astro`).
3. **Shapes with jobs, the hexagon for the diamond.** Riot's square structures, its diamond guides the
   eye, its circle holds the items "that require a reaction": in champion select "Pick intent selections
   are displayed in the main (circular) portrait of the player", bans in "a separate square portrait"
   ([wiki](https://wiki.leagueoflegends.com/en-us/Team_drafting#Champion_Selection)); levels sit in teal rings, actions on
   round buttons, and small diamonds mark portraits and options. Aubaine keeps the square (the 44 px step
   and help squares, the 38 px pause square) and puts the pointy-top hexagon of its logo where Riot puts
   the diamond, as the owner asked on 2026-10-16: before the kind (`SheetHead.astro`), as the group mark
   (`SelectScreen.astro`), as the rank profile's rings and vertex marks (`RankProfile.astro`,
   `src/lib/game/profile.ts`) and as the seal's crest (`CoverSeal.astro`). No approved piece marks anything
   with a circle or a ring; the one round control is the drafts switch (`FilterBar.astro`). Riot's case
   for a ring is the ready check: a reaction asked during a real wait.
4. **Light marks state and progress.** Riot draws its magic for "timers, activity indicators, and
   progress bars": the ready check's blue arc running round its ring, the selected mode's emblem filling
   with blue light, teal arcs on level rings, the Battle Pass line lit up to the level reached. In Aubaine
   the light is the living stage: its shafts (`src/scripts/art-effect/glsl/stage.frag`) are cast from
   above the focal card, which `data-stage-focus` marks on the sheet's card (`CharacterHero.astro`) and on
   the spotlight's card (`SpotlightPanel.astro`). For new pieces, light may mark a state or a real wait,
   and the state is also said in words: nothing is told by colour alone. Riot's queue timer does set
   small status words in teal ("Estimated: 2:40"); the community palette keeps its teal Blue 3
   (`#0AC8B9`) for "gradients and progression bars" and out of type.
5. **The stage belongs to the selected thing.** After lock-in, champion select's loadout screen fills its
   middle with the selected skin's splash, blurred out to the window's edges; the wiki: "The selected
   skin's splash art is shown on screen." A season's art fills the Battle Pass, TFT's painting fills its
   tab. The approved select screen makes the same move: the stage takes the pointed entry's blurred cover
   or, without a cover, its seal's colours; a mouse selects after 80 ms of intent, focus selects at once,
   touch never selects by pointing, and the stage cross-fades in 450 ms (`src/scripts/select-screen.ts`,
   `src/scripts/art-effect/stage.ts`). Champion select is the Hextech precedent for that stage swap;
   Deadlock's hero select is the other ([deadlock.md](deadlock.md)). A hub does not copy it: the Codex
   built as a select screen was turned down on 2026-10-28; the Codex opens on the plate menu instead and
   Livres on its contents (`approved-pieces.md`).
6. **A kind is a colour, a mark, a name and a line.** Each rune path has a colour, an icon, a name and one
   line ("Improved attacks and sustained damage" for Precision,
   [wiki](https://wiki.leagueoflegends.com/en-us/Rune)); the Runes tab's filter is the path icons, each in
   its colour, the current one white over a short gold bar. Aubaine gives each kind of content and each
   book category one colour and one pictogram (`KIND_MARKS` and `CATEGORY_MARKS` in
   `src/lib/game/kind-marks.ts`; the owner picked the category marks on 2026-10-30) and a name and a
   one-line tagline (`src/lib/i18n/strings.ts`). The rule: the name always sits beside the mark.
7. **Gold for the secondary controls.** Riot's secondary controls are gold: gold arrowheads on the skin
   carousel, a round gold close button beside CONFIRM, UNLOCK and PURCHASE RP in gold outlines, and so are
   CHANGE MODE and LEARN MORE. Aubaine's approved secondary controls follow it: the spotlight's step
   arrows are 44 px dark squares under a faint gold rule with gold chevrons (`Spotlight.astro`), the help
   button is the same square tilted 3° (`TitlePlates.astro`), the pause button a 38 px square whose icon
   stays grey until pointed or focused (`StillToggle.astro`). Aubaine's primary action is not blue magic:
   « Ouvrir la fiche » is a plate of gold paper, as are the first title plate and the Filtres button:
   Persona 5's paper ([persona-5.md](persona-5.md)), not Hextech's metal.
8. **The utility layer stays plain.** In the Items tab rows carry no box, on and off are lit and dark teal
   icons, and disabled buttons sit in grey frames. Aubaine's select screen keeps its filter row quiet:
   fields under the faint gold rule, the result count on a black tape, one gold plate for Filtres
   (`FilterBar.astro`); notes open in a native popover on a dark ground under a thin violet rule
   (`PopoverNote.astro`). A tab bar, a column of tabs or a band of tiles is never a hub's design: the
   2026-10-29 prototypes that dressed that layer in thin gold lines (ledger, collection, sections, livres
   bookmarks, livres hextech histories) were rejected.
9. **Same frames, different worlds.** The TFT tab of 2019 sets TFT's own purple painting behind the
   client's banners, gold outlines and thin progress rings; the Battle Pass of 2025 brings each season's
   art into the same frames; the champion overview, the loadout and the Store's lead offer are each built
   on a skin's splash art. That is the owner's "same DA but different" (2026-10-28) for the hubs: the
   house language stays, and each hub differs by the pictures, colours and composition it already owns,
   never by a picture or a video made for it (2026-10-27).
10. **One motion vocabulary.** Riot shares one set of timings and easings and lets the easing tell
    priority ([How Riot built it](#how-riot-built-it)). Aubaine shares one easing token, `--ease`
    (`src/styles/fonts.css`). In the approved primitives the slap curve `cubic-bezier(0.2, 1.45, 0.4, 1)`
    is written out three times (`DescriptorStickers.astro`, `TraitPlates.astro`, `TitlePlates.astro`) and
    five more curves are literals. Paper slaps with an overshoot (Persona 5's part); cards, slabs and light
    settle, which is Riot's soft landing. Riot's opacity-and-transform rule is the rule for Aubaine's new
    arrivals; two approved motions animate other properties: the rank profile unfolds by animating its SVG
    path (`RankProfile.astro`) and the ghost name drifts by its margin (`GhostName.astro`).
11. **Magic as a layer over a still stage.** Riot baked its magic and swapped large videos for still
    pictures in low-spec mode. Aubaine draws its magic live instead: the WebGL2 context the art effects
    share (`src/scripts/art-effect/studio.ts`) paints the entry's own picture, or its seal's colours, as
    drifting smoke, rising motes and light shafts, at 30 drawn frames a second on the ultra tier, 24 on
    high and none on low (`EFFECT_TIERS` in `src/lib/art-effect/presets.ts`). What it shares with Riot is
    the fallback: the CSS ambient of `StageBackdrop.astro` is the whole stage whenever no canvas is drawn,
    and the stage holds still under reduced motion or the pause button (`src/scripts/art-effect/stage.ts`).
12. **In-game feel, web layout.** The client's feel is made with web techniques, which supports the
    owner's ruling of 2026-10-28: "We are building a website with the VIBE and FEEL of a video game and a
    DA close to Persona 5 / Deadlock / Hextech. We are not BUILDING A VIDEO GAME."

## What Aubaine leaves

- **The client as a device.** Riot's question produced a language for every screen, not a drawn device.
  Reading it as a device led to the Codex drawn as the Almanach's CADRAN screen: a navy screen with a
  grid, HUD corners and a gold frame, and a status row reading LIAISON CODEX and EN LIGNE. The owner,
  2026-10-30: "I see what you
  meant about CADRAN and thing, but the problem is once you enter Skill Trees for exemple well it's just
  not the same vibe so i prefere keeping the vibe of our current Skill Trees and Species." and "the
  clearly Aubaine DA (and not CADRAN DA, that are different)". The CADRAN look belongs to the Atlas boot
  only (`src/scripts/atlas-boot.ts`,
  `docs/adr/0044-the-cadran-boot-is-the-atlas-loader-and-its-bar-follows-the-real-load.md`);
  « Codex » stays the name of the indexes; the prototype's files were deleted on 2026-10-30.
- **The launcher's chrome.** The social column, the currencies, PLAY and the main tabs frame every client
  screen but champion select, which hides them. Aubaine keeps its own site header above the sheet and the
  select screen.
- **Blue as the action colour.** Riot's blue magic marks the game's own actions: PLAY, CONFIRM, FIND
  MATCH, ACCEPT!, LOCK IN. Aubaine's one primary action is gold paper, and blue is an identity colour like
  any other: Humain's authored key `#44a8e7` (`data/species/humain.json`), the Règles kind `#3f86b8`. No
  colour is reserved for the Almanach (settled on 2026-10-30). Whether a teal light marks progress or
  state anywhere on the site is still the owner's call.
- **The diamond.** Riot's progressive accent marks portraits, lines and options; Aubaine's is the
  pointy-top hexagon.
- **The featured panel over a row of captioned tiles.** It is the Home of patch 7.24; the rejected home
  client prototype of 2026-10-29 copied it.
- **Inventories, brackets and wheels.** The Loot tab, where loot is sorted and converted, Clash's bracket
  and the emote wheel are game layouts (the Loot tab and the bracket not viewed). Aubaine's own radial
  command fan was turned down on 2026-10-28 and rejected outright on 2026-10-30; its slanted doors,
  turned down the same day, came back at the owner's request and are kept unshipped as layout D
  (`prototypes.md`).
- **Riot's typefaces.** [Fonts In Use](https://fontsinuse.com/uses/26935/league-of-legends-game-and-website)
  (Stephen Coles, 23 June 2019) reads the serif as Beaufort, modified by Monotype, and the sans as Spiegel
  Sans, in the game and on the websites; Riot's articles above do not name them. Aubaine sets names in
  Cinzel 700, kinds in JetBrains Mono capitals, stickers and buttons in Barlow Semi Condensed, subtitles in
  EB Garamond italic and reading text in Spectral (`src/styles/fonts.css`).

## Seen, not taken yet

None of these is approved or tested.

- **The current item marked by metal and light**: the client's top navigation marks its current tab with
  a small gold chevron hanging from the window's top rule and a soft light under the word. Aubaine's
  header underlines the current item in house gold (`data-nav-underline` in
  `src/components/shell/Header.astro`).
- **Badges that hang off a card's edge**: mastery crests off the cards' lower-left corners, the Store's
  ribbons off their top-left corners. Aubaine's roster badges sit inside the frame (`CoverCard.astro`: a
  status badge 14 px from the top-left corner, a draft's stamp in the middle, the characteristic hexagons
  12 px from the top-right).
- **A straight track for steps in order**: the Battle Pass levels, the 2019 TFT pass. A candidate for a
  book's chapters or a booklet's versions.
- **A route of hexagon nodes**: the rune paths of League's rune page and recommender, with each node drawn
  as Aubaine's hexagon, as Riot's 2016 alpha rune page drew its rune slots
  ([Added on 2026-10-31](#added-on-2026-10-31)). A direction for a hub, never built.
- **Doors as emblems or banners**: a mark with its own silhouette, its name and its line, lit when chosen
  (mode select), or banners hanging in one row (lobby). Neither has been built: the Codex opens on the plate
  menu and Livres on its contents (`approved-pieces.md` § The hub plate menu, § The Livres contents), whose
  doors each carry the same pointy-top hexagon mark. Hexagon emblems set on cards and tiles, rather than doors, were part of two rejected hubs
  (2026-10-28 and 2026-10-29, `rulings.md` § Hubs).

## Added on 2026-10-31

A second research pass rechecked the 42 links above (all answered) and went to Riot's own words and the
client's screens. What it adds:

- **Riot's 2016 dev blogs survive in mirrors.** The client update's alpha site is gone
  (`na.leagueoflegends.com/en/client-update/` answers 404, and the Wayback Machine kept empty pages), but
  Surrender at 20 mirrored its posts: "Hextech Brings the Magic: Defining a Visual Style" (17 May 2016,
  seven months before The Visual Language of Hextech), "Crafting the Client: The Play Loop" (4 May 2016)
  and "Crafting the Client: Profile and Collection" (2 June 2016), with Riot's answers in Q&As. Their
  figures were viewed. The mirrors do not name the authors, so their words are attributed to Riot Games.
- **A Hextech precedent for the hexagon.** Riot's own client drew pointy-top hexagons twice: every rune
  slot on the 2016 alpha rune page (outlined in the rune type's colour, larger for the top class, dark
  when empty, dark with a padlock when locked) and the reward markers on the 2019 ranked split track.
  The diamond stays Riot's usual accent, but Aubaine's hexagon has a precedent in Hextech itself.
- **Screens now viewed** from the old still-to-view list: the client in 2025 (Riot's /dev post on the
  2025 seasons), the rune collection, page and recommender, the emote wheel, the Honor ballot and
  checkpoint, the profile in 2018 and 2024, and Clash's 2018 beta lobby and scouting. Still not viewed:
  the Loot tab itself, Clash's bracket, the 2025 profile, and the PLAY and ready-check animations.
- **The mastery crests changed.** The January 2024 dev post shows orbs in a gold ring; the support page
  of 31 July 2025 shows matte gold crests with gems from level 6. Neither says why.
- **The GDC 2017 talk.** The Vault's video record (`play/1024096`) is the same session as the slides
