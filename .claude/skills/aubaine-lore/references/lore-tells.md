# Lore tells

The lens of `aubaine-lore-editor` and part of the lens of `aubaine-lore-critic`. Long narrative prose
has tells of its own, beyond the ones `.claude/skills/aubaine-prose/references/ai-tells.md` holds for
all prose, which both run too. The transplantable paragraph is `ai-tells.md` § Specificity, and the
template heading is `ai-tells.md` § Shape.

## Whose text it is

When the page starts from the owner's text, a tell in that text is a `note` for the owner, never an
edit. In a revision, the owner's text changes only as `lore.md` § Start from the owner's text
allows, and the frozen list is never touched.

## The tells

Long narrative prose has tells of its own, and one early Scothan draft had all of them.

- A catalogue of possibilities: sentence after sentence of « un clan pouvait… », « un greffier
  peut… ». Say what happened, to whom.
- The paired contrast on repeat: welcomed at one table, searched at the next door, praised at the
  guild, stopped at the bridge. Once can be the point; three times is a tic.
- The wry aside, a knowing quip at the reader's elbow (« les érudits préfèrent la première
  traduction, les ivrognes la dernière »). Lore is told from inside the world, not winked at.
- One motif hammered: the same image in every section, a clerk and his register, a bolted door.
  Give each section its own.
- The archetype word: one word that dresses the people as a stock type, spread across the page, as
  « druidique » was in five parts of the elf page. Name what the thing is; when the word also gives
  the people a magic or anything else `architecture.md` § Lore and mechanics names, that section
  applies.
- The list title: a chapter title that lists what the chapter holds, as « Chants, rites et lames »,
  the sign of paragraphs that share nothing but the title. `architecture.md` § What each part is for
  says where each paragraph goes.
- The epitaph closer. Every section of a history is tempted to end on a line fit for a tombstone;
  most should simply stop. Its ominous variant ends on a threat or a prophecy nobody in the canon
  made: the shadow that still watches, the debt that will be paid. Its widening variant ends by
  leaving the section's subject for the whole people, the age or the world.
- The marked norm, in text a writer adds: a people's own institution shown as an exception to the
  reader's world (« pourtant », « même », « contrairement à », "unlike", "surprisingly"), or a
  player's part that describes it by what it withholds from one gender. Describe it by its offices,
  as the way things are. The owner's own sentences are never flagged by it.
- Fake mystery: a sentence that promises significance and gives no reason to care, such as « nul ne
  sait vraiment pourquoi » or « un secret que seuls les anciens connaissent » with nothing in the canon
  behind it. A real mystery rests on something (`lore.md` § Mystery); this one is decoration.
- The stock arc: they suffered, then they reclaimed their name. Tell the specific history, and let
  the arc emerge from it or not at all.
- The reach-ahead: a sentence that borrows what another section of the same page tells, as an
  opening that says the living fear the dead « depuis l'histoire de Bruford » when a Bruford section
  follows. Inside « Histoire » each fact lives in its own section; elsewhere, just say what holds.
  On a species page, the opening tells no event, and the opening and « En jeu » may still restate a
  fact lightly (`architecture.md` § What each part is for and § One home per fact).

Three more show up when a history is written rather than told:

- Significance inflation: « témoigne de », « marque à jamais », « symbole de », a detail promoted
  to a meaning nobody in the world states. Say what the detail is, and let the reader weigh it.
- The vague witness: « on dit que », « certains racontent », « les anciens affirment » with no one
  named. Name who says it, or state it as fact if the canon holds it.
- The brochure: « niché », « riche », « millénaire » piled on a place or a people. One concrete
  particular carries more than three adjectives. Its other form is the sweep, a phrase that covers a
  whole people or a whole land at once where one named place, person or act would carry it.
- The puffed copula: « constitue », « s'impose comme », « représente » where « est » says the same
  thing. « se veut » that attributes a claim to its holder is not this.
- The empty coinage: a word a writer invents that names nothing the ordinary word does not. The
  ordinary word stays. The owner's own coinages are the owner's.
- The yardstick, in text a writer adds: another people used to measure this one, as a height given
  against a human's or a colouring called unusual beside a human norm. It puts the other people at
  the centre of this people's page. One clause of contrast that ends on this people is not this tell.
- The unasked sentence: a sentence on a page of the owner's that no words of the owner asked for, however
  true its fact. It is a `PROPOSE` row, never an improvement (`lore.md` § Start from the owner's
  text).

Three more show up when the owner's answers are copied into the page. An answer settles the canon
for the writer; it is not text for the reader. The owner refused all three on the Humain page on
2026-10-26:

- The echoed answer: a sentence that exists because the owner answered a question, such as a
  negative nobody claimed (« Al-Wahaa n'en est pas membre », when nothing suggested it was).
- The implied consequence: a sentence the reader already draws from the one before, such as « Les
  rois et les reines de la Nouvelle-Aubaine sont des Colbert » after Arthur became its first king.
- The stray fact: a fact set down where it serves nothing, such as the Oracles' human-only rule
  between two sentences on the Weitzguard. It goes in the section whose subject it governs.

## Stock openings and stock frames

These read as any fantasy setting's, in either language. They are diagnostics, never a banned list:
the problem is the formula a writer reached for instead of a fact, and the finding says what fact
the sentence should have carried.

- « Depuis la nuit des temps », « Au cœur de leur culture », « Un peuple profondément lié à »,
  « Plus qu'une simple », « Entre tradition et », « Une harmonie fragile », « Le poids des siècles »,
  « Leur héritage témoigne de ».
- "Since time immemorial", "An ancient and mysterious people", "Deeply connected to nature", "More
  than mere", "A delicate balance between", "A testament to", "Woven into the fabric of", "Yet beneath
  the surface".

## Checks over the whole page

- Read every section's last sentence in a row. If most end on a polished line, let some of them
  stop.
- Read every section's first sentence in a row. If they share a grammatical shape, rewrite one.
- Count the antitheses and the « pouvait », the triplets, and the trailing glosses (a clause added to
  a finished sentence to say what it shows). Each one has to earn its place; judge the clusters, never
  a single word.
- Read the French and the English as two texts, each written for its reader.
- Read the page's quote beside every other species quote: no shared question, opening, image or
  ending shape.
- Read « Jouer » beside the sections it sits above. It may restate their facts lightly
  (`architecture.md` § One home per fact); a paragraph that retells a section is the finding.
