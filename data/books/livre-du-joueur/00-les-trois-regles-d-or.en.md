---
title: "The three golden rules"
description: "The three rules that come before every other rule in Aubaine: the Rule of Cool, customisation and good faith."
---

Aubaine rests on three golden rules. They come before every other rule, including the ones in this book, and every page that follows answers to them.

> [!PRINCIPLE] The three golden rules
>
> 1. **The Rule of Cool.** In Aubaine, anything cool is allowed, even when it bends the fiction or a rule. Cool prevails. But the Uncool is never Cool.
> 2. **The Rule of Customisation.** Players and GMs are welcome to change Skills, items and anything else to give each player a unique experience and let them play whatever they want. Renaming a Skill and changing its damage type and Domain to fit a frost wizard is no problem at all, as long as the player honours the third rule.
> 3. **The Rule of Good Faith.** GMs and players are all here to play and enjoy Aubaine. A player who abuses Aubaine's fiction-first philosophy, or the kindness of their GM, has no place at a roleplaying table. Dressing something up as cool just to min-max your character is Uncool.

## Cool

The first rule gives the table the right to put a great scene ahead of the letter of a rule. An impossible leap from a balcony, a line that turns a duel around, an idea nobody saw coming: if the whole table finds it cool, it happens, and the GM works out how to resolve it.

Cool is judged by the whole table; one player's excitement is not enough. Whatever spoils the evening for the others, flattens someone else's character or breaks the tone the table agreed on is not cool, and the rule does not cover it.

## Customisation

Nothing in Aubaine is set in stone. A Skill can change its name, its damage type or its Domain to suit the character you want to play, and an item can take another shape or another story. The GM approves each change, and the character sheet records it.

The more a change touches what a Skill does at the table, its power or its price, the more the third rule weighs on the decision.

## Good faith

The third rule guards the other two. Cool and customisation both rest on trust between players and GM, and that trust does not survive abuse.

A player acting in good faith suggests an idea because it makes the scene better. A player acting in bad faith hunts for loopholes, dresses up an optimisation as a cool idea, or wears the GM down until they give in.

When in doubt, ask yourself one question: would what I am asking for make the game better for everyone?
