Creating a character takes eight steps, in order. The first seven are decisions, the last is arithmetic. Do not fill in a single numbered box before step 8: a character is imagined first and counted afterwards.

Take your time over the early steps. Who is this character, where do they come from, what drives them onto the road: the answers will guide every number you write down later.

## What the GM gives you

A new character receives:

- one Domain Tree;
- one Archetype Tree;
- two Species Skills;
- 25 Experience Points (XP);
- 22 Mastery Points (MP);
- 3 Potential Points (PP).

The GM announces any change to that set before the table starts. A one-evening game and a long campaign do not open on the same numbers.

## Three currencies

They do not convert into one another. A currency spent in the wrong place is only recovered in play.

XP
: Buys Trees, Skills and their Levels.

MP
: Buys the six {{caracteristique|Characteristics}}, the {{aptitude|Aptitudes}} and the {{specialite}}.

PP
: Buys the three resource paths: Vitality, {{memoire}} and {{energie}}.

## The order of the steps

1. **Species, Archetype, Domain.** Choose them from what the GM offers, and your subspecies or regional origin where the Species has any. They fix your first two Trees, your two Species Skills and your {{vitesse}}.
2. **The {{ame}}.** Write down the {{phobie}}, the {{manie}}, the {{defaut}}, the {{specialite}} and the {{don}}.
3. **{{caracteristique|Characteristics}}.** Spend MP.
4. **{{aptitude|Aptitudes}} and {{specialite}}.** Spend MP.
5. **Resources.** Place the 3 PP.
6. **Skills.** Spend the 25 XP in your Trees.
7. **Equipment.** Fill the eight worn slots.
8. **Derived values.** Work out {{pdv}}, {{memoire}}, {{energie}}, {{ca}}, {{vitesse}} and Initiative.

A step can be replayed as long as nobody has rolled a die. After the first {{jet}} of the first session the sheet is fixed, and progression takes over.

The Species chapter covers step 1 in detail, and the Trees and Skills chapter covers step 6.

## The Soul

The {{ame}} is what sets a character apart once the numbers are equal. It is decided with the GM, in whatever tone the table has set for itself.

Every character carries five pieces of it.

{{phobie}}
: A fear that weighs on their decisions. It earns its place when it costs something.

{{manie}}
: A habit, an obsession, a gesture that keeps coming back.

{{defaut}}
: A lasting trait that draws trouble towards them.

{{specialite}}
: A narrow personal field they excel in.

{{don}}
: An exceptional faculty that belongs to them alone.

The first three make the character's life harder and earn them {{karma}}. The last two say what they can do like nobody else at the table.

### Karma

The {{phobie}}, the {{manie}} and the {{defaut}} each carry a {{karma}} slot. A character therefore holds at most three points, one per slot.

The GM grants a {{karma}} point when they judge it deserved, often when one of those three traits has cost the character something, and chooses the slot. A slot already filled does not take a second. {{karma}} you hold disappears neither at a rest nor at the end of a session.

Spend a point before a {{jet}}, or after the throw while the result is still unresolved. Each point adds an {{avantage}} or a {{desavantage}} to the {{jet}} of your choice: yours, an ally's, an opponent's. Several points can land on the same {{jet}}.

A point spent after the throw adds its die to the ones already on the table, and the two kept dice are then worked out again across the lot. That is what lets you rescue a failed {{jet}}, and equally what lets you ruin a successful one when the point lands on an opponent.

Some Skills take {{karma}} as their resource. That is why they are rare in play.

### The Speciality

The {{specialite}} is an {{aptitude}} you invent, with a deliberately narrow field. It is the trade, the lore or the passion in which your character outdoes everyone: "Painting", "Traps", "Bombs", "Trade", "Cooking", "Navigation", "Monsters". It goes up with MP like any other {{aptitude}}.

When it applies, it enters the {{jet}} like an ordinary {{aptitude}} and grants an {{avantage}} on top. The {{caracteristique}} still follows how you act: a "Cooking" {{specialite}} rolls with {{esprit}} to recognise a spice by taste, and with {{dexterite}} to set out a banquet in an hour.

A broad field gives a lukewarm bonus that helps often; a narrow one gives a real push that helps rarely. The GM settles the edge cases.

> [!EXAMPLE] Example
>
> Oswin has the {{specialite}} "Trade" at +2 and nothing in {{persuasion}}. At the market he haggles over a saddle: the action falls within his field, and the GM asks for a {{charisme}} + Trade {{jet}} with 1 {{avantage}}.
>
> That evening, he has to talk a guard into letting him through after curfew. There is nothing to buy or sell: the {{jet}} goes back to {{charisme}} + {{persuasion}}, and Oswin has only his {{charisme}} to count on. If he slips the guard a coin, the GM can decide they are back to haggling.

### The Gift

The {{don}} is an exceptional faculty that belongs to your character alone: a capability, a perception or a peculiarity. Write it as one sentence, in the first person: "My paintings come to life", "I can speak to the dead", "I receive premonitions", "I know when I am being lied to".

It opens up information, permission or an opportunity another character would not have.

Information
: Someone who can speak to the dead questions a witness's corpse. What the dead one knows, and what they agree to say, is the GM's to decide.

Permission
: A character with this {{don}} sees their paintings come to life, and a painted bird can fly away. Nobody else can even attempt it.

Opportunity
: Premonitions go through the GM, who chooses when a vision comes and what it shows. What you do with it is yours.

When a consequence of the {{don}} has an uncertain outcome, it resolves with the ordinary rules. The {{don}} does not touch {{jet|Rolls}}, damage, healing, {{ca}}, resources or your number of actions, unless another rule bought separately says so.

Settle it with the GM before the first session: what it allows, and how far it goes. The Rule of Good Faith weighs heaviest here: a {{don}} written to get round the rules, rather than to say who your character is, is Uncool.

> [!EXAMPLE] A Gift and a Speciality
>
> Maëlle's {{don}} is "My paintings come to life" and her {{specialite}} is "Painting". Locked in a cell, she paints a sparrow on the wall with a lump of charcoal.
>
> The {{don}} makes it possible, and the GM accepts that the bird comes to life. Painting a sparrow true enough to fly, in charcoal and by the light of an arrow slit, is still uncertain: the GM asks for a {{dexterite}} + Painting {{jet}}, with 1 {{avantage}} from the {{specialite}}. Maëlle succeeds, and the sparrow flies out between the bars with the message she has entrusted to it.
>
> A painted wolf would come to life too, but it would only truly bite with a Skill that provides for it: the {{don}} deals no damage.

## Mastery Points

The six {{caracteristique|Characteristics}} and every {{aptitude}} start at 0. Your 22 MP raise them. They are the two halves of every {{jet}}, and they are paid for in the same currency.

A {{caracteristique}} costs its new value. An {{aptitude}} costs half its new value, rounded up. Both cost at least 1.

| New value | {{caracteristique}} | {{aptitude}} |
| ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 1 | 1 | 1 |
| 2 | 2 | 1 |
| 3 | 3 | 2 |
| 4 | 4 | 2 |
| 5 | 5 | 3 |
| 6 | 6 | 3 |

The cost is cumulative: taking a {{caracteristique}} from 0 to +3 costs `1 + 2 + 3`, so 6 MP. The same climb on an {{aptitude}} costs `1 + 1 + 2`, so 4 MP.

At creation a {{caracteristique}} caps at +3 and an {{aptitude}} at +2. In play both reach +6.

You may drop a {{caracteristique}} or an {{aptitude}} to −1. Each value lowered this way frees 1 MP to spend elsewhere. A −1 is paid on every {{jet}} that uses it, and since the GM chooses the pair, you do not control when it lands.

A {{caracteristique}} serves everywhere it appears, an {{aptitude}} only in its own field, and a {{caracteristique}} costs twice as much. The question is therefore not which is better, but how many different {{jet|Rolls}} you want to be able to attempt.

### The six Characteristics

{{force}}
: The character's physical power, the kind that lifts, pushes and strikes.

{{dexterite}}
: The character's coordination and precision, in gesture as in movement. Carries {{ca}} and Initiative.

{{constitution}}
: The character's health and endurance, what lets them take punishment and hold on. Carries {{pdv}}.

{{intelligence}}
: The character's ability to analyse, remember, learn and deduce. Carries {{memoire}}.

{{esprit}}
: The character's attention and empathy, the sharpness of their senses and their bond with the supernatural. Carries {{energie}}.

{{charisme}}
: The character's presence, their way of expressing themselves and the authority they carry.

Four of them carry a derived value. That does not make them more important: a {{caracteristique}} with no derived value serves every {{jet}} that uses it, which happens far more often.

### The Aptitudes

The sheet carries the full list, one box per {{aptitude}}, and the Rules page defines each of them. They cover what a character can do, from tracking to negotiation.

Four of them are also the four crafting disciplines: {{artisanat}}, {{arcanes}}, {{technologie}} and {{science}}. They are one and the same idea: the discipline a piece is worked under is the {{aptitude}} you roll to make it.

Six come up more than the rest, and they are the combat ones: {{melee}}, {{finesse}} and {{visee}} to land a blow, {{reflexes}}, {{resistance}} and {{volonte}} to resist one.

One more {{aptitude}} joins the list, and it belongs to you alone: your {{specialite}}, introduced with the {{ame}}. It is paid for in MP at the same prices and with the same caps as the others.

## Resources

Each Potential Point raises one of the three paths by one level. They are independent and each caps at level 10.

Vitality
: `{{pdv}} = 20 + 2 × {{constitution}} + 5 × Vitality Level`

{{memoire}}
: `{{memoire}} = 4 + {{intelligence}} + {{memoire}} Level`

{{energie}}
: `{{energie}} = 8 + {{esprit}} + {{energie}} Level`

Your 3 PP go wherever you like: three paths at one level, or one path at three.

{{memoire}} is the room you have for your Skills. A {{memorisee}} Skill takes 1 {{memoire}}, whatever its Level and its cost. Your two Species Skills are {{memorisee}} without taking up any {{memoire}}, as the Species chapter explains, and the ones your equipment grants take up nothing. Apart from your two Species Skills, you can change your {{memorisee}} Skills during a {{repos-court}} or a {{repos-long}}.

{{energie}} pays for the Skills that cost something. A Skill whose cost exceeds your reserve cannot be played. An attempt whose conditions are not met consumes nothing.

## Spending the 25 XP

XP buys the Skills of your Trees and their Levels, and unlocks new Trees. Each purchase costs 5 to 100 XP, in steps of 5, and the price is printed on the Skill.

In a Tree, the first Skill you buy is its heart, at the centre; every purchase after that must be joined by a line to a Skill you have already {{apprise}}. The Trees and Skills chapter explains the rule in full.

Twenty-five XP does not make a complete character. It makes an opening: the heart of a Tree and one or two cheap neighbours that say how your character acts in the first round. The rest comes in play.

Buy what you will play in every fight first. An expensive Skill far from the heart will wait until the table has paid for it.

## The six values to carry over

These six boxes are the only ones that are calculated. Work them out again after every purchase of MP or PP.

| Box | Formula |
| --- | --- |
| {{pdv}} | `20 + 2 × {{constitution}} + 5 × Vitality Level` |
| {{memoire}} | `4 + {{intelligence}} + {{memoire}} Level` |
| {{energie}} | `8 + {{esprit}} + {{energie}} Level` |
| {{ca}} | `12 + {{dexterite}}` unarmoured, otherwise the armour's formula |
| {{vitesse}} | 9 metres, unless your Species says otherwise |
| Initiative | `1d4 + {{dexterite}}`, rolled at the start of combat |

Armour replaces the {{ca}} formula instead of adding to it. A raised shield and the other explicit modifiers are added afterwards.

## A worked example

Sélène is a scout, more at home in the woods than in town, who tracks what others miss. Her player writes her {{ame}} first, with the GM.

{{phobie}}
: Deep water.

{{manie}}
: She counts her arrows before she sleeps.

{{defaut}}
: She trusts nobody in town.

{{specialite}}
: "Tracking".

{{don}}
: "I receive premonitions".

Her 22 MP:

| {{caracteristique}} | Value | MP |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| {{dexterite}} | +3 | 6 |
| {{constitution}} | +2 | 3 |
| {{esprit}} | +2 | 3 |
| {{intelligence}} | +1 | 1 |
| {{charisme}} | 0 | 0 |
| {{force}} | −1 | −1 |

Twelve MP in all. She accepts the −1 in {{force}}: she does not push doors open, she goes around them.

| {{aptitude}} | Value | MP |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| {{visee}} | +2 | 2 |
| {{discretion}} | +2 | 2 |
| {{reflexes}} | +2 | 2 |
| {{perception}} | +1 | 1 |
| {{survie}} | +1 | 1 |
| {{specialite}} "Tracking" | +2 | 2 |

Ten MP, and the count lands exactly on 22.

Her 3 PP go one to each path. Her derived values:

- {{pdv}}: `20 + 2 × 2 + 5 × 1` = **29**
- {{memoire}}: `4 + 1 + 1` = **6**
- {{energie}}: `8 + 2 + 1` = **11**
- {{ca}}: `12 + 3` = **15** unarmoured
- {{vitesse}}: **9 m**
- Initiative: `1d4 + 3`

Her 25 XP go into the heart of her Domain Tree and one cheap Skill joined to it. Two Skills to {{memorisee|memorise}} against six {{memoire}} slots, since her Species Skills take none: she has room to grow before she has to choose.

On a shooting {{jet}} she throws `2d12 + 3 + 2`. When she tracks, her {{specialite}} enters the {{jet}} and gives her an {{avantage}} on top. And when danger waits further down the trail, her {{don}} can show her a glimpse of it before she gets there, if the GM so decides.
