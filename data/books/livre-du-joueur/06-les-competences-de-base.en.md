title: "Basic Skills"
description: "What every creature can do, with no XP, no Memory and no Energy: fighting, defending, hiding, helping, and improvising the rest."
---

Some Skills belong to nobody. Every creature has them, from the first round of the first session to the end of the campaign. They cost no XP, take up no {{memoire}}, ask for no {{energie}}, and no Tree sells them.

Together they make the list of what anyone can always attempt, with or without a Tree: {{ATTAQU-001}}, {{COURIR-001}}, {{CACHER-001}}, {{AIDERX-001}}, {{ESQUIV-001}} and the others. The Skills index carries the full list.

## Reading an entry

They read like the Skills in your Trees: the name, then what the Skill takes to play, its range and its duration, then its rule. They carry no price and no box to tick, since there is nothing to buy and nothing to {{memorisee|memorise}}.

An entry marked "1 {{attaque}}" is not played on its own: it replaces one of the Attacks of the {{ATTAQU-001}} action. That is the case for manoeuvres such as {{BOUSCU-001}} and {{AGRIPP-001}}, which knock down or hold an opponent instead of wounding them.

## What they cover

Most of them serve in combat. There is something to strike with, something to defend with and something to get away with: {{ESQUIV-001}} makes you harder to hit, {{DESENG-001}} lets you leave an opponent without offering an {{Attaque d'opportunité}}, and {{PREPAR-001}} holds an action back for the moment something happens.

Others serve everywhere. {{CACHER-001}} and {{CHERCH-001}} work against each other, in an alley as much as on a battlefield, and {{AIDERX-001}} gives a hand to an ally attempting something you could do yourself.

## Improvising

The list opens with {{IMPROV-001}}, which covers whatever the others do not.

When nothing covers what you are attempting, say what your character wants and how they go about it. The GM answers three things: whether it is possible, what the attempt costs and risks, and which {{jet}} settles it.

> [!EXAMPLE] Example
>
> **Player:** "I throw my knife at the chandelier's rope, so it comes down on the guards."
>
> **GM:** "You can, and it takes your {{action}}. Make a {{dexterite}} + {{visee}} Roll to cut the rope at the right moment. If you succeed, the guards underneath make a Dexterity + {{reflexes}} Roll against your {{dd}}, or they are knocked {{a-terre}}."

This list is not closed. It describes what tables do most often; anything the fiction allows plays out the same way, and the Rule of Cool counts here more than anywhere.
