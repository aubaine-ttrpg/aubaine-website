Combat is the most detailed part of the game. Most of a game, though, is spent exploring: a crypt to search, a forest to cross, a door nobody dares to open. All of it runs on the ordinary loop. You describe what you do, the GM sets a Roll and a DC when one is needed, and the result settles it.

This chapter gathers the rules for the situations that come up at every table.

Your Gift and your Speciality often find their place here. A Gift for speaking to the dead gets the skeleton in a crypt to testify, a Gift of premonitions can warn you of the corridor about to give way, and a "Traps", "Navigation" or "Monsters" Speciality enters the Rolls these places call for.

## Seeing and being seen

Much of what an adventurer attempts depends on what they can see: spotting danger, aiming at a target, reading an inscription. Light, and whatever gets in the way of sight, changes the Rolls.

### Light

Bright light
: Full daylight, an overcast sky, the circle of a torch or a campfire. You see normally.

Dim light
: Dawn, dusk, a full moon, the edge of bright light fading into the dark. You see poorly.

Darkness
: A moonless night, the back of a cave, a room with the shutters closed. You see nothing.

### Visibility

Reduced visibility
: In dim light, a light fog or thick foliage, Rolls that depend on sight, such as most Perception Rolls, take 1 Disadvantage.

No visibility
: In darkness, a thick fog or opaque smoke, you see nothing that is inside it. For anything you try to see in that area, you are [[Aveuglé]].

Some Skills and some Species let you see where others see nothing. Their text says exactly what they change.

## Hiding and searching

To slip out of sight, use {{Se cacher}}. You need something to hide behind: cover, shadow, the back of a creature busy with something else. The GM says whether the situation allows it. On a success, you gain [[Caché]] against the creatures you fooled.

To flush out a hidden creature, or find something that eludes you, use {{Chercher}}. A secret passage, a trap or a clue can be found as long as you search in the right place: if you search far from what is hidden, no result reveals it.

> [!EXAMPLE] Example
>
> The party is searching a necromancer's library. Sélène's player says she is examining the shelves at the back, where the dust is missing. The GM asks for a Spirit + Perception Roll against DC 20. She succeeds: one book sounds hollow when she pulls it, and the shelf swings open. Another player, who was searching the desk, would have found nothing whatever they rolled.

## Travel

Over an adventure, characters cover long distances, across hours or days. The GM can sum up a journey in a few sentences, or play it step by step when the road matters.

### Pace

Outside combat, a group chooses its pace. It sets the distance covered and what the group notices along the way.

| Pace | Per minute | Per hour | Per day | Effect |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | --- |
| Fast | 120 m | 6 km | 45 km | Perception, Survival and Stealth Rolls take 1 Disadvantage. |
| Normal | 90 m | 4.5 km | 36 km | Stealth Rolls take 1 Disadvantage. |
| Slow | 60 m | 3 km | 27 km | Perception and Survival Rolls gain 1 Advantage. |

A day of travel counts eight hours on the move. On mounts, a group can cover twice the distance for one hour, after which the mounts need a Short Rest or a Long Rest before they can keep up that pace again.

### Marching order

Before setting off, the group says who walks in front and who brings up the rear. That order decides who springs a trap, who spots a danger first and who is in reach when a fight breaks out. It can be changed freely outside combat.

### Keeping your bearings

On a road, nobody gets lost. Off the paths, in fog or deep forest, the GM can ask for a Spirit + Survival Roll to hold your course. A failure costs time, supplies, or an encounter the group would rather have avoided.

## Handling objects

Pulling a lever, opening a chest, lighting a lantern: most interactions resolve by describing them, with no Roll. The GM says what happens.

Breaking a fragile object, such as a vial or a sheet of paper, takes an Action and succeeds without a Roll. For something sturdy, a barred door or a chain, the GM sets a Roll, most often Strength + Athletics, and its DC according to how tough the object is.

As long as the fiction stays believable, your pack holds what you put in it; when it stops being believable, the GM will tell you.

## Dangers

Monsters are not the only threat. The world itself wounds, starves and freezes those who venture into it unprepared.

### Falling

A creature that falls takes 1d6 Bludgeoning damage for every 3 metres it falls, up to 20d6. If it takes at least 1 damage, it lands [[À terre]].

A creature that falls into water or another liquid can use its Reaction to make a Strength + Athletics or Dexterity + Acrobatics Roll against DC 15. On a success, it enters the liquid cleanly and takes only half the damage.

### Breath

A creature can hold its breath for `1 + Constitution` minutes, and at least 30 seconds.

When its breath runs out, it can last another `1 + Constitution` rounds, and at least 1. At the start of its next turn, it drops to 0 HP and gains [[Agonie]], as the Damage and healing chapter describes. It cannot recover anything until it breathes again.

### Hunger and thirst

A creature that has not eaten or drunk enough during a day recovers neither HP nor Energy at the end of its next Long Rest. It recovers normally once it has eaten and drunk its fill.

Beyond a few days of going without, the GM sets the consequences, according to what the fiction demands.

### Cold and heat

A creature exposed without protection to extreme cold or heat makes a Constitution + Resistance Roll against DC 10 at the end of each hour. On a failure, it takes 1d6 Cold or Fire damage, whichever is wearing it down.

Suitable clothing, shelter or a Skill that guards against those temperatures removes the Roll or makes it easier. The Skill's or the piece's text says how.

## Loot

After a significant encounter, or on finding a source worth working, each character receives a Loot Opportunity. The Equipment chapter explains how to spend it.
